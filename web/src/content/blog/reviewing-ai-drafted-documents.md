AI tools can take hours out of first drafts and first-pass reviews. But the value of that time saving depends entirely on the quality of the review that follows. The lawyer's professional obligations do not change because a tool was involved.

Here is a practical checklist we recommend for any AI-assisted legal output.

## 1. Confirm the inputs

Output is only as good as the information behind it. Before reviewing the result, check that the facts, parties, jurisdiction and commercial terms supplied to the tool were complete and correct.

## 2. Trace every conclusion to source text

For review tools, each flag should point to a specific clause. Read the clause itself rather than relying on the summary. For drafting tools, check that the draft reflects the instructions you gave.

## 3. Check what's missing, not just what's there

AI output can be persuasive precisely because it is well structured. Ask what a careful lawyer would expect to see that isn't present: a carve-out, a notice provision, a jurisdiction-specific requirement.

## 4. Apply your own judgement on risk

Risk ratings are a starting point for prioritisation. Whether a deviation is acceptable depends on the client's commercial context, bargaining position and risk appetite — factors only you can weigh.

## 5. Protect confidentiality

Use only tools whose data handling you understand and that meet your professional and contractual obligations. Confirm whether data is retained, where it is stored and whether it is used to train models.

## 6. Keep a record

Where appropriate, document how AI-assisted work was reviewed and approved. This supports quality control and helps demonstrate appropriate supervision.

## The bottom line

Well-designed legal AI should make verification faster, not optional. The goal is not to trust the tool — it's to make checking its work quick enough that you always do.
